# Do Spaced-Repetition Apps Actually Improve Long-Term Retention?

**Researched:** 2026-09-13
**Question:** Is there solid evidence that spaced-repetition apps (Anki, SuperMemo, etc.) improve long-term retention compared to restudying?

---

## TLDR

Yes — the testing effect and spacing effect are among the most replicated findings in cognitive psychology, and spaced-repetition apps operationalise both. The main caveats: much of the evidence comes from lab settings with simple material, effect sizes shrink with complex material, and the time cost only pays off when retention over weeks/months actually matters.

## The Core Evidence

Retrieval practice beats restudying for long-term retention. In a landmark experiment, students who took practice tests recalled ~50% more a week later than students who restudied the same material ([Roediger & Karpicke, 2006](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1111/j.1467-9280.2006.01693.x)).

Spacing amplifies this. A major review of ten learning techniques rated *practice testing* and *distributed practice* as the only two techniques with "high utility" out of ten evaluated — ahead of highlighting, rereading, and summarising, which rated low ([Dunlosky et al., 2013](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1529100612453266)).

Spaced-repetition apps like Anki implement an expanding-interval algorithm derived from the [Ebbinghaus forgetting curve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forgetting_curve) (1885), scheduling each review just before predicted forgetting.

## Where the Evidence Gets Shaky

- **Lab-vs-classroom gap.** Much of the foundational work used word lists and nonsense syllables. Classroom studies show smaller effects, and some fail to find advantages over well-implemented restudying for complex material ([review discussion](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1529100612453266)).
- **Material complexity matters.** Benefits are strongest for discrete facts (vocabulary, anatomy, law definitions). Conceptual, skill-based learning shows weaker transfer from spaced retrieval alone.
- **Time cost.** For exams days away, cramming can outperform spacing because the material doesn't need to survive weeks. Spacing wins specifically when retention must last.
- **Community experience is mixed on workload.** Heavy users report card-making time can exceed review time, which is a real efficiency loss if cards are written badly — a recurring theme in [community discussion](https://www.reddit.com/r/medicalschoolanki/).

## Practical Bottom Line

For factual domains where retention over months matters (languages, medicine, law, religious text memorisation), spaced repetition is the best-evidenced technique available. For one-off exams, complex conceptual understanding, or skill acquisition, treat it as one component rather than a complete method.
